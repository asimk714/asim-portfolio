Experience

Product thinking that grew out of real technical delivery.

I don't claim a Product Manager title I didn't hold. What I claim is the product judgment that grew out of 8+ years inside complex, cross-functional technical delivery — navigating ambiguity, translating requirements into execution, coordinating across stakeholders, and delivering cross-functional work on complex systems in the carrier network, optical, and IP domains at Nokia.

At Nokia, I worked on complex technical systems — Fixed Networks, IP, and Optical — where engineers don't get the luxury of fuzzy requirements. You read the system, translate vague directives into buildable technical execution, coordinate across multiple teams, and stay honest about what you don't know.

That environment taught me the things a product thinker needs before anything else: how to read a complex system, how to talk to engineers about constraints without pretending to be one, how to translate a vague requirement into something buildable, and how to stay honest about what I don't know.

One example of that product-adjacent judgment at Nokia: a customer site repeatedly raised outage-related tickets. After the issue persisted through initial troubleshooting, the team replaced hardware and returned the suspected card to the repair centre. My involvement came after multiple customer complaints. We recreated the field environment at the repair centre and compared unit behaviour between the field site and the controlled environment. The investigation identified sudden power surges causing network cards to reboot, and I proposed an alternate continuous-power solution. I participated in the investigation and recommendation; I did not implement the power infrastructure myself.

Outcome note: the same customer has not raised the same complaint in roughly two years. This is based on my experience and reporting and is not independently measured in this portfolio.

The three projects on this portfolio are the proof that this way of working transferred into product work. Each one is a real product I built end-to-end — with its own measurement, verification, and honest gaps. They are not hypotheticals. They run.
