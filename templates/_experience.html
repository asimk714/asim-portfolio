One example of that product-adjacent judgment at Nokia: a customer site repeatedly raised outage-related tickets. After the issue persisted through initial troubleshooting, the team replaced hardware and returned the suspected card to the repair centre. My involvement came after multiple customer complaints. We recreated the field environment at the repair centre and compared unit behaviour between the field site and the controlled environment. The investigation identified sudden power surges causing network cards to reboot, and I proposed an alternate continuous-power solution. I participated in the investigation and recommendation; I did not implement the power infrastructure myself.

Outcome note: the same customer has not raised the same complaint in roughly two years. This is based on my experience and reporting and is not independently measured in this portfolio.

GPON Fixed Network — technical ownership across stakeholders

On the GPON Fixed Network system, the operational workflow follows a clear chain. A Site Reliability Engineer (SRE) is responsible for establishing a site and checking whether it is running smoothly. When a problem or operational situation occurs, the SRE escalates to the relevant stakeholders or managers depending on the issue and the affected board or component. GPON Fixed Network contains different types of boards and components, and the right contact depends on which part of the system is involved.

My responsibility in this environment is fault finding, root-cause analysis (RCA), troubleshooting, and dispatching OK / working units. The work spans both hardware and software. I have strong knowledge of the hardware, and when site problems, hardware problems, or repeated issues occur, people contact me because of that hardware knowledge.

The work therefore involves interaction across an operational environment: SREs and site operations on the front end, relevant stakeholders and managers in the escalation path, and repair-centre or technical personnel during troubleshooting and unit dispatch. The workflow, broadly: an operational or site issue comes in, the relevant stakeholder is contacted depending on the affected board or component, fault finding begins, hardware and software troubleshooting follows, RCA is carried out, and a working / OK unit is dispatched.

One recurring incident in that environment illustrates the investigation approach. A site kept going down, and I was involved from the beginning after the GPON ticket was raised. Because I regularly handle customer escalations and RCA, I proposed bringing the complete field setup to the Repair Center with the available diagnostic outputs.

The investigation established that an NT card was resetting. That could corrupt the flash card holding configuration, ultimately causing the FX-8 shelf to reset. At the Repair Center, I first reproduced the field configuration and then isolated individual boards when the setup initially appeared healthy. Under the field configuration, I observed a slight reset on one NT card, replaced it, and confirmed that the setup then operated normally.

This work involved the customer-handling team, SRE, technical personnel, and the Repair Center. I also use a troubleshooting/RCA checklist I developed from experience to narrow recurring problems before moving into component-level isolation.

Operational context: GPON site tickets and returned-unit history are recorded through the operational process, with DOA, repaired-faulty, and repeat-failure cases receiving deeper investigation. This example documents the observed investigation and repair action; it does not claim that I personally performed a formal long-term recurrence analysis for this site.

Evidence classification: observed. This describes the operational environment, my responsibility, and the stakeholder chain as they exist in the work — not a product strategy, roadmap, GTM, user research, or business KPI role.
