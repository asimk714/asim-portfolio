PROOF, NOT ADJECTIVES Nokia Systems RCA

A customer's site kept going down. I recreated the field setup at the repair centre, traced the outages to power surges rebooting network cards, and proposed a continuous-power fix. According to my reporting, the customer has not raised the same complaint in roughly two years; that period has not been independently measured.

1
Customer problem: Recurring site dropouts and repeated card reboots threatening carrier SLA uptime.
2
Recreated conditions: Bench-replicated field topology and electrical load at the repair centre.
3
Root cause identified: Transient power surges triggering hardware reset cycles on installed network cards.
4
Proposed corrective action: Continuous-power regulation and updated site power configuration procedures.
5
Outcome & limitation: Customer has not raised the complaint in roughly two years (self-reported; not independently measured).
Why Product Management

I realized that the fixes and solutions I was proudest of were never the cleverest ones — they were the ones that began with a persistent question: what is this failure mode actually costing the person on the other end? Answering that in roadmaps, PRDs, and user journeys is Product Management.

How I Solve Problems

I don't start with abstract slides. I start by experiencing the friction myself at the critical moment — a checkout at peak concurrency or an AI drafting assistant mid-sentence — and diagnosing exactly where the experience fails. Then I work backward to the underlying architecture.

How I Align Teams

I lead with evidence before authority. In complex hardware and software incidents, getting cross-functional teams to align requires placing the failure mode and reproducible data on the table, not personal opinion. Evidence is the only thing that convinces without needing a title.

Working Style

I build tangible, interactive prototypes and verifiable test suites rather than relying on decks. I operate in tight loops: build the smallest real version of an idea, stress-test it against edge cases, and let empirical measurement dictate the next round of investment.

Decision Making

My decision frameworks are deliberate and transparent. I reach for RICE, MoSCoW, or Impact-vs-Effort matrices because they ground trade-offs in visible criteria that any engineer, designer, or business partner can inspect and challenge.
