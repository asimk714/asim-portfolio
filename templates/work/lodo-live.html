Why this matters

In a single-source lending flow, the borrower sees one offer and must trust it. In LoDo's concept, the borrower saw multiple normalized offers, compared total cost components, and made an explicit choice. The acceptance is recorded as an auditable action in the prototype.

What LoDo is trying to change

MSME borrowers often receive a single offer from a single lender and have no structured way to compare alternatives on the same terms. LoDo's concept is a reverse auction: the borrower creates one structured intent, and multiple lenders bid on it.

Consent + verification (design intent)

The portfolio positions LoDo around consent and verification so that borrowers understand what is being shared and what has been verified before they see offers. This web demonstration does not implement an explicit consent or verification step — that is a product-design direction, not a feature of this prototype.

Illustrative data

Lender names, APR values, fees, and total repayment figures shown here are illustrative demo data from the source prototype's seed data. They are not real offers and do not represent real lender terms.

Market context (not a LoDo outcome)

Some LoDo descriptions reference a $300B MSME credit opportunity. That figure is market/context information, not a LoDo outcome, not a validated addressable-market measurement for this prototype, and not a claim about captured share.
