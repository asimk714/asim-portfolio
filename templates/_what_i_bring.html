What I Bring to Product Teams

Not in spite of the engineering. Because of it.

I bring 8+ years of engineering experience and formal product management training to product work — the kind of background that helps me define a product before building it, translate between product intent and engineering constraints, and tell the difference between a measured outcome and a claim I've talked myself into. I'm pursuing Technical Product Manager, Product Owner, and AI PM roles where that combination matters.

{% include "_icons/define-measure-verify.html" %}

Product judgment

I define the problem before I build the solution. I can sit with a vague brief and turn it into a product question that engineers can actually build against — usually before the first line of code is discussed.

{% include "_icons/tech-bracket.html" %}

Technical depth

I can translate between product intent and engineering constraints — APIs, systems, data, integration points, failure modes. I do not need an engineer to tell me whether an idea is feasible before I can reason about it honestly.

{% include "_icons/measurement-sticky.html" %}

Measurement discipline

I define what success means before I claim I achieved it. Where I can't measure, I say so. Where I've only talked myself into a conclusion, I say so too — because otherwise the claim isn't worth trusting.

{% include "_icons/evidence-tag.html" %}

Evidence discipline

I separate what I observed, what I verified, what I inferred, what I hypothesized, and what I proposed. If a result is not yet validated, I put it with the hypothesis and the next step — and I do the same for the gaps in the case study, not just the wins.

The portfolio is built to make that visible quickly — the judgment, the technical depth, the measurement, and the honesty about what isn't validated yet.
